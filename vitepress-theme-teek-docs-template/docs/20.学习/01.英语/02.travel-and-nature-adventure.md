## Chapter One: The Departure

Every journey begins with a single belief: that beyond the familiar boundary of daily life, **something beautiful waits to be discovered**. My brother and I had shared this dream since childhood, and on a bright morning in early June, we finally set out to chase it.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>每一段旅程都始于一个单纯的信念：在日常生活那熟悉的边界之外，有某种美好的东西正等待着被发现。</p>
<p>我和哥哥自童年起就共享着这个梦想，而在六月初一个晴朗的早晨，我们终于出发去追寻它。</p>
</div>

### Two Brothers, One Dream

The trip had been months in the planning. I had found a bargain on a boat booking through a travel blog, and my buddy, who worked as a broadcaster for a local broadband network, had agreed to come along. We packed our bags with basic supplies: a tent, a blanket, a battery-powered lamp, a camera, and enough food to last a week. Our budget was tight, but we were both certain that the experience would be worth every penny.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>这次旅行已经筹划了好几个月。</p>
<p>我通过一个旅行博客淘到了预订船只的特价，而我的哥们——他在一家本地宽带网络公司做播音员——已经答应一同前往。</p>
<p>我们在包里装上了基本物资：一顶帐篷、一条毯子、一盏电池灯、一台相机，以及足够维持一周的食物。</p>
<p>我们的预算很紧，但我们俩都确信，这段经历会值每一分钱。</p>
</div>

I woke early that day in my small bedroom and lay in bed listening to the clock tick on the wall. The ceiling above me was bare, and a single beam of sunlight fell across the floor. I could hear my brother in the bathroom, running water for a bath. He was always careful about these things — punctual, organized, the kind of person who made a checklist for everything. I, on the other hand, was more casual, prone to last-minute decisions and careless mistakes. But together, we made a good combination.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>那天我在我的小卧室里早早醒来，躺在床上听着墙上时钟的滴答声。</p>
<p>我头顶的天花板空无一物，一束阳光斜斜地落在地板上。</p>
<p>我能听见哥哥在浴室里放水准备洗澡。</p>
<p>他向来在这些事上很细心——守时、有条理，是那种会为每件事列清单的人。</p>
<p>而我就不同了，比较随性，容易在最后一刻做决定，也容易犯粗心的错误。</p>
<p>但合在一起，我们是很好的组合。</p>
</div>

My boss had given me the week off — a rare gift from a man who seemed to regard leisure as a character flaw. I sat in a chair by the window, fumbling with the buttons of my shirt. On the cabinet beside me lay a birthday card from my girlfriend — my birthday had been the week before — and a cheap bar of chocolate I had been saving. I tossed a crumpled receipt into the bin and checked my phone. One message from my colleague: "Do not forget to settle the bill before you leave." Another from a client, asking me to clarify the terms of a contract. I would deal with it later. Today, the only thing that mattered was the journey.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>老板给了我一周的假——这来自一个似乎把闲暇视为性格缺陷的人，实属难得的恩赐。</p>
<p>我坐在窗边的椅子上，笨手笨脚地扣着衬衫的纽扣。</p>
<p>我身旁的柜子上放着一张女朋友送的生日贺卡——我的生日就在上周——还有一块我一直留着没吃的便宜巧克力。</p>
<p>我把一张揉皱的收据扔进垃圾桶，然后看了看手机。</p>
<p>一条来自同事的消息：“别忘了走之前把账结清。”</p>
<p>另一条来自一位客户，让我把一份合同的条款说清楚。</p>
<p>我打算之后再处理。</p>
<p>今天，唯一重要的事情就是这趟旅程。</p>
</div>

By the time I went downstairs, my brother had already laid out breakfast on the kitchen bench: bread, butter, cheese, and a pot of coffee. He had also packed a basket of provisions for the trip: rice, beans, dried beef, biscuits, chocolate, and a bottle of cooking oil. It was not the diet of a chef, but it would sustain us. On the table beside his plate sat a small cake he had bought from a cafe the day before — a bonus treat for the road.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>等我下楼时，哥哥已经把早餐摆在了厨房的台面上：面包、黄油、奶酪，还有一壶咖啡。</p>
<p>他还为这趟旅行装好了一篮子给养：大米、豆子、牛肉干、饼干、巧克力，还有一瓶食用油。</p>
<p>这可不是大厨的膳食，但足以让我们维持下去。</p>
<p>他盘子旁的桌上放着一个小蛋糕，是他前一天从一家咖啡馆买的——路上额外的一点犒赏。</p>
</div>

"We should calculate our expenses carefully," he said, biting into a piece of toast. "I do not want to run out of cash halfway through."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“我们得仔细算算开销，”他咬着一片吐司说。</p>
<p>“我可不想半路上把钱花光。”</p>
</div>

I nodded. "Do not bother. I have already done the calculation. We have enough, besides what I have set aside for emergencies."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我点了点头。</p>
<p>“不用费心了。</p>
<p>我已经算过了。</p>
<p>除了我留出来应急的钱，我们足够了。”</p>
</div>

"In case of what?" he asked, raising an eyebrow.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“以防什么？”</p>
<p>他挑起一根眉毛问道。</p>
</div>

"Any circumstance," I said. "You never know."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“以防任何情况，”我说。</p>
<p>“你永远说不准。”</p>
</div>

He looked at me with a hint of caution. "You are sure?"

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>他带着一丝谨慎看着我。</p>
<p>“你确定吗？”</p>
</div>

"Certainly," I said. "Trust me."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“当然，”我说。</p>
<p>“相信我。”</p>
</div>

He pulled out a calendar and checked the dates one more time. "The weather forecast looks good. Clear skies for the next five days."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>他掏出日历，又把日期核对了一遍。</p>
<p>“天气预报看起来不错。</p>
<p>接下来五天都是晴天。”</p>
</div>

"Then let us not waste the chance," I said. "Let us go."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“那我们就别浪费这次机会了，”我说。</p>
<p>“我们走吧。”</p>
</div>

We locked the door behind us and walked to the bus stop. A neighbour waved from across the street, and I waved back. "Bye!" she called. "Have a good trip!"

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们锁上身后的门，走向公交站。</p>
<p>一位邻居从街对面挥手，我也挥手回应。</p>
<p>“再见！”</p>
<p>她喊道。</p>
<p>“旅途愉快！”</p>
</div>

### Out of the City

The bus arrived on time, and we boarded, finding seats near the back. The streets were still dark, and a cold wind blew through the open windows. My brother checked his map one last time — a classic paper map, not a digital one — while I stared out at the buildings passing by. The capital was a city in the grip of a boom: construction everywhere, cranes looming over old neighbourhoods, property prices climbing into the billions. A local candidate for parliament had plastered his face on every corner, promising to boost the economy and cure cancer if elected. His campaign was clearly well funded, but nobody believed a word of it. The citizens had heard it all before.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>公交车准时到达，我们上了车，在靠后的位置找了座位。</p>
<p>街道还很昏暗，冷风从敞开的车窗灌进来。</p>
<p>哥哥最后一次查看他的地图——一张传统的纸质地图，不是电子地图——而我则望着窗外掠过的建筑出神。</p>
<p>首都正处在蓬勃发展的热潮中：到处都是工地，起重机矗立在老旧街区之上，房价攀升到数十亿的规模。</p>
<p>一位本地的议会候选人把他的面孔贴满了每个街角，承诺一旦当选就要提振经济、治愈癌症。</p>
<p>他的竞选显然资金充足，但没有人相信他说的一个字。</p>
<p>市民们早就听过这一套了。</p>
</div>

We passed a church, a clinic, and a row of shops in the commercial district. A clerk stood outside a chain of convenience stores, arranging a display of canned goods and a brand of local beer. Next door, a clothing store displayed winter coats in the window, and a fast-food restaurant filled the air with the smell of fried chicken. A man on a bicycle rode past, narrowly avoiding a boy on a bike. Another man stood on the corner smoking a cigarette, his blonde hair catching the early light. We passed the university campus, where a banner advertised a basketball tournament and a screening of a comedy at the campus cinema. Somewhere nearby, I could hear the faint strains of classical music drifting from a music class. The civil servants who worked in the government buildings were already at their desks, navigating the endless bureaucracy of forms and codes. A former chairman of the city council was in the news again, facing charges of corruption. He had tried to cancel the investigation, but the evidence was too strong. In the old part of the city, a medieval castle stood on a hill. A delivery carrier unloaded boxes at its gate.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们经过一座教堂、一家诊所，以及商业区里的一排店铺。</p>
<p>一名职员站在一家便利店连锁店外，摆放着罐头食品和某个本地啤酒品牌的陈列。</p>
<p>隔壁，一家服装店在橱窗里展示着冬装外套，一家快餐店则用炸鸡的香味弥漫了整个空气。</p>
<p>一个骑自行车的男人驶过，险险地避开了一个骑单车的男孩。</p>
<p>另一个男人站在街角抽着烟，金色的头发迎着清晨的光线。</p>
<p>我们经过大学校园，那里有一条横幅在为一场篮球赛和校园影院的一部喜剧片放映做宣传。</p>
<p>在附近的某处，我能听见从一间音乐教室飘来的古典音乐若有若无的旋律。</p>
<p>在政府大楼里办公的公务员们已经坐到了桌前，应付着没完没了的表格与规章构成的官僚体系。</p>
<p>市议会的一位前主席又上了新闻，面临腐败指控。</p>
<p>他曾试图撤销调查，但证据太过有力。</p>
<p>在城市的旧城区，一座中世纪的城堡矗立在山丘上。</p>
<p>一名送货员在城堡门口卸着箱子。</p>
</div>

I adjusted my belt and settled into my seat. It was the beginning of a long day for the city, and I was glad to be leaving it behind, because the comfortable routine of urban life had begun to feel like a burden. In the basement of an office building, I caught a glimpse of a cable broadcast studio — my buddy's workplace, where he produced a daily commentary on current affairs. The whole city was in competition with itself, every business trying to outdo the next, every businessman rushing to beat his competitor. It was a competitive world, and the civilians who lived in it rarely had time to pause and breathe. I looked down at my clothes — old jeans, a worn jacket — and thought how different they would look by the end of the week.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我调整了一下腰带，在座位上坐定。</p>
<p>对这座城市来说，漫长的一天刚刚开始，而我庆幸自己正把它抛在身后，因为城市生活那种舒适的日常已经开始像一种负担。</p>
<p>在一栋办公楼的地下室里，我瞥见了一间有线广播演播室——那是我哥们的工作场所，他每天在那里制作时事评论。</p>
<p>整座城市都在与自己竞争，每家企业都想超过下一家，每个商人都急匆匆地想击败自己的对手。</p>
<p>这是一个充满竞争的世界，生活在其中的普通人很少有时间停下来喘口气。</p>
<p>我低头看了看自己的衣服——旧牛仔裤、一件磨旧的夹克——心想到了这周末它们会变得多么不同。</p>
</div>

"Did you hear the news?" my brother said, lowering his voice. "There was a bombing in the east. They think it was a bomb planted by rebels."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“你听说那条新闻了吗？”</p>
<p>哥哥压低了声音说。</p>
<p>“东部发生了一起爆炸。</p>
<p>他们认为那是一名叛军安放的炸弹。”</p>
</div>

"That is terrible," I said.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“太可怕了，”我说。</p>
</div>

"The government claims they will catch whoever is responsible. But I do not think anyone will be held to blame. It never is."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“政府声称他们会抓住肇事者。</p>
<p>但我觉得不会有人被追究责任。</p>
<p>从来都是这样。”</p>
</div>

I shook my head. The world was full of chaos and conflict, and sometimes it felt like the only choice was to close your eyes and carry on. But that was not why we were here. We were here to go somewhere beyond all of that — somewhere wild and clean and free.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我摇了摇头。</p>
<p>世界充满了混乱与冲突，有时感觉唯一的选择就是闭上眼睛，继续前行。</p>
<p>但那并不是我们来到这里的原因。</p>
<p>我们来这里，是要去往超越这一切的某个地方——一个原始、洁净而自由的地方。</p>
</div>

At the train station, we transferred to a car — a borrowed vehicle, since my own was in the shop with a broken engine. My colleague from work had offered to lend it to me, and I was grateful. The car was old but capable, and it carried us steadily toward the coast. We drove past coal mines and industrial buildings, past a canal that once served as a transport route for barges and barrels of goods, past fields where cattle grazed beside stone walls. The landscape changed gradually, the grey of the city giving way to the green and brown of the countryside.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>在火车站，我们换乘了一辆汽车——一辆借来的车，因为我自己的车因发动机故障还在修理厂。</p>
<p>我的同事主动提出把它借给我，我很感激。</p>
<p>这辆车虽旧但很能跑，稳稳地把我们带向海岸。</p>
<p>我们驶过煤矿和工业建筑，驶过一条曾用作驳船和货物木桶运输通道的运河，驶过牛群在石墙旁吃草的田野。</p>
<p>景色渐渐变化，城市的灰暗让位给了乡野的绿与褐。</p>
</div>

My brother, who had a degree in biology from college, pointed out features of the landscape as we drove. "See that river bend?" he said. "The water quality there is a good benchmark for the whole region. They monitor it on a regular basis."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>哥哥在大学里拿过生物学学位，一路上他为我们指点着沿途的地貌特征。</p>
<p>“看见那个河湾了吗？”</p>
<p>他说。</p>
<p>“那里的水质是整个地区的一个很好的基准。</p>
<p>他们定期对它进行监测。”</p>
</div>

"You sound like a teacher in a classroom," I said.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“你听着就像教室里的老师，”我说。</p>
</div>

He laughed. "Old habits. I used to coach kids at the community club on weekends. Biology, chemistry, basic science. I loved it. Every child who came in was a blank page, ready to learn."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>他笑了。</p>
<p>“老习惯了。</p>
<p>我以前周末在社区俱乐部给孩子们做辅导。</p>
<p>生物、化学、基础科学。</p>
<p>我很喜欢。</p>
<p>每个进来的孩子都是一张白纸，随时准备学习。”</p>
</div>

"Maybe that should be your career," I said. "Instead of sitting in an office being bored."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“也许那应该成为你的职业，”我说。</p>
<p>“而不是坐在办公室里百无聊赖。”</p>
</div>

"Maybe," he said. "Maybe."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“也许吧，”他说。</p>
<p>“也许。”</p>
</div>

We crossed a bridge over a river, and the road climbed into hills. At one point, we passed a sign marking the border between two districts. "We are officially out of the city now," my brother said, and I felt a rush of excitement.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们驶过一座跨河的桥，道路开始向山丘攀升。</p>
<p>在某个地方，我们经过了一块标明两个区边界的路牌。</p>
<p>“我们现在正式出城了，”哥哥说，我顿时一阵兴奋。</p>
</div>

## Chapter Two: The Coast

By midday we reached the coast. The sea stretched out before us, blue and boundless, and the sight of it made my heart beat faster. A small cabin near the beach served as our meeting point. Inside, a cheerful man with a brown beard and a baseball cap sat behind a wooden bench, sorting through a basket of fish. He was the captain of the boat we had booked, and he greeted us with a broad smile and a charm that put us immediately at ease.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>正午时分，我们到达了海岸。</p>
<p>大海在我们面前绵延展开，湛蓝而无边，那景象让我心跳加快。</p>
<p>海滩附近的一间小屋是我们的会合地点。</p>
<p>屋里，一个留着棕色胡子、戴着棒球帽的爽朗男人坐在一张木制长桌后，正翻拣着一篮鱼。</p>
<p>他就是我们预订的那条船的船长，他用灿烂的笑容和一股令人立刻放松下来的亲和力迎接我们。</p>
</div>

"You are the boys heading to the island?" he asked. We nodded. "Good. The tide is in our favour. We can commence the journey within the hour."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“你们就是要去岛上的那两个小伙子吧？”</p>
<p>他问。</p>
<p>我们点了点头。</p>
<p>“好。</p>
<p>潮水对我们有利。</p>
<p>我们一小时内就能出发。”</p>
</div>

### Waiting on the Beach

While we waited, we sat on the beach and watched the waves. The sun was climbing, and its beams cast a golden glow across the water. A few birds circled overhead, and I watched them dive and bounce through the air with a grace that seemed almost biological in its precision. A cat prowled along the shoreline, hunting for scraps. Nature had a way of putting everything into perspective. The beauty of it was overwhelming — the kind of beauty that makes you stop and breathe and feel grateful just to be alive.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>等待的时候，我们坐在海滩上看海浪。</p>
<p>太阳渐渐升高，阳光在水面上投下一片金色的光晕。</p>
<p>几只鸟在头顶盘旋，我看着它们俯冲、在空中起伏，那种优雅仿佛达到了近乎生物本能的精准。</p>
<p>一只猫沿着海岸线悄悄踱步，寻觅着残羹剩饭。</p>
<p>大自然总有办法让一切归于恰当的尺度。</p>
<p>它的美令人难以招架——那种让你停下脚步、深呼吸，并为活着而心怀感激的美。</p>
</div>

"I could sit here all day," I said, taking a deep breath.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“我可以在这儿坐上一整天，”我深吸一口气说。</p>
</div>

My brother leaned back on his elbows and closed his eyes. "Let us enjoy it while we can. Once we are on that boat, we are at the command of the sea."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>哥哥用胳膊肘撑着向后靠，闭上了眼睛。</p>
<p>“趁还能享受就享受吧。</p>
<p>一旦上了那条船，我们就得听凭大海的摆布了。”</p>
</div>

### Setting Sail

The captain's boat was a sturdy vessel, its hull built to withstand the rough waters of the channel. We climbed aboard, stowing our gear at the bow, beneath the deck. The engine coughed once, then burst into life, and a small bell rang as we pulled away from the dock. The sea was calm, and the engine's rhythm seemed to blend with the sound of the water. The coastline gradually shrank behind us, and soon there was nothing but open sea on every side. We were bound for the island, and there was no turning back.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>船长的船十分结实，船体造得足以经受海峡的汹涌水域。</p>
<p>我们爬上船，把装备收在船头甲板下面。</p>
<p>发动机咳了一声，随即轰然启动，我们离开码头时一只小铃铛响了起来。</p>
<p>海面平静，发动机的节拍似乎与水声融在了一起。</p>
<p>海岸线在我们身后渐渐变小，很快四周只剩下开阔的大海。</p>
<p>我们朝着那座岛驶去，再无回头之路。</p>
</div>

I had brought a book about the island's history — a biography of the explorer who had first charted its shores. The book described a civilization that had thrived there for centuries, a colony of fishermen and farmers who lived in harmony with the land. Their culture was based on a deep respect for nature, and their ceremonies celebrated the changing of seasons, the birth of children, and the bounty of the harvest. It was a fascinating read, and I found myself lost in its chapters as the boat cut through the water.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我带来了一本关于这座岛历史的书——一位最早测绘其海岸的探险家的传记。</p>
<p>书中描述了一个曾在那里繁盛数百年的文明，一个与土地和谐共处的渔民与农夫的聚居地。</p>
<p>他们的文化建立在对大自然的深深敬畏之上，他们的仪式庆贺季节更替、孩子降生，以及丰收的恩赐。</p>
<p>这是一本引人入胜的书，船破水前行时，我不知不觉沉浸在了它的章节里。</p>
</div>

My brother, meanwhile, was busy with his camera. He had a gift for capturing beautiful images, and he photographed everything: the spray of the waves, the clouds building on the horizon, the captain's hands on the wheel. He was in his element, and I could see by the bright look in his eyes that he was happy.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>与此同时，哥哥正忙着摆弄他的相机。</p>
<p>他有一种捕捉美丽影像的天赋，他拍下了一切：飞溅的浪花、在地平线上堆积的云、船长握着舵轮的手。</p>
<p>他如鱼得水，从他眼中明亮的神采我看得出他很快乐。</p>
</div>

At one point, the captain pointed to a spot on his chart. "See that?" he said. "That is where the old canal used to run. The colonial government built it to transport coal from the mines. Now it is mostly blocked. Nature took it back."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>某个时候，船长指着海图上的一处。</p>
<p>“看见那个了吗？”</p>
<p>他说。</p>
<p>“那条老运河当年就从那里流过。</p>
<p>殖民政府修它是为了从矿上运煤。</p>
<p>如今它大都被堵住了。</p>
<p>大自然把它收了回去。”</p>
</div>

"Everything comes back to nature eventually," my brother commented.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“一切最终都会回归自然，”哥哥评论道。</p>
</div>

The captain nodded. "Basically, yes. You can build all you want, but in the end, the land decides what stays."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>船长点了点头。</p>
<p>“基本上，是的。</p>
<p>你想建什么都可以，但到最后，是土地决定什么能留下。”</p>
</div>

After about three hours, the island appeared on the horizon — a dark shape rising from the sea, crowned with green trees and a central peak that towered above the rest. As we drew closer, I could make out the details: a sandy beach, a cluster of palm trees, and the faint outline of a stone building near the shore. The captain steered the boat into a small bay, and we dropped anchor.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>大约三小时后，那座岛出现在地平线上——一个从海中升起的深色轮廓，顶部覆盖着绿树，中央一座山峰高耸于其余一切之上。</p>
<p>随着我们靠近，我能辨认出细节：一片沙滩、一丛棕榈树，以及海岸附近一座石砌建筑的模糊轮廓。</p>
<p>船长把船驶入一个小海湾，我们抛下了锚。</p>
</div>

"Welcome," he said, "to one of the last wild places on earth."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“欢迎，”他说，“来到地球上最后几片荒野之一。”</p>
</div>

## Chapter Three: The Island

We stepped onto the sand and felt the solid ground beneath our boots. The air was warm and sweet, carrying the scent of tropical flowers and the faint smell of salt. A path led from the beach into the bush, and we followed it, pushing through branches and bending low beneath low-hanging leaves. The forest was alive with sound — the buzz of bees, the call of birds, and the rustle of unseen creatures in the undergrowth. I spotted a butterfly resting on a blade of grass, its wings a bizarre and beautiful colour, like a living jewel.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们踏上沙滩，感到靴底踩着坚实的地面。</p>
<p>空气温暖而甜美，带着热带花朵的芬芳和淡淡的咸味。</p>
<p>一条小径从海滩通向灌木丛，我们沿着它走，拨开枝条，在低垂的树叶下弯腰前行。</p>
<p>森林里充满了声响——蜜蜂的嗡嗡声、鸟儿的鸣叫，以及下层灌木中看不见的生物发出的窸窣声。</p>
<p>我发现一只蝴蝶停在草叶上，它的翅膀呈现出奇异而美丽的颜色，像一颗活着的宝石。</p>
</div>

### Setting Up Camp

We set up camp in a small clearing beside a stream. The water was clear and cold, running over smooth stones and falling into a natural basin. My brother hung a cloth from a branch to create some shade, and I began to unpack our supplies. We had brought a small camping stove, a pot, and a box of provisions. It was not fancy, but it would sustain us.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们在溪边一块小空地上扎了营。</p>
<p>溪水清澈而冰凉，流过光滑的石头，落入一个天然的水潭。</p>
<p>哥哥在树枝上挂起一块布来遮阴，我则开始把我们的物资拿出来。</p>
<p>我们带来了一只小露营炉、一口锅，还有一箱给养。</p>
<p>算不上讲究，但足以让我们维持下去。</p>
</div>

That first evening, as the sun set in a brilliant display of orange and purple, we sat by our fire and listened to the sounds of the forest. The blaze crackled and cast dancing shadows on the trees. Somewhere in the distance, a beast — perhaps a wild boar or something larger — moved through the bush, snapping branches as it went. A bat flitted overhead, its small body darting through the gathering dark. I felt a moment of caution, a brief flash of fear, but it passed. We were here to embrace the wild, not to hide from it.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>第一个傍晚，当太阳以橙与紫的绚烂景象落下时，我们坐在火堆旁，倾听着森林的声音。</p>
<p>火焰噼啪作响，在树上投下摇曳的影子。</p>
<p>在远处的某处，一头野兽——也许是野猪，也许是更大的东西——穿过灌木，一路折断着枝条。</p>
<p>一只蝙蝠在头顶掠过，它小巧的身躯在渐浓的夜色中穿梭。</p>
<p>我有过一瞬间的谨慎，一阵短暂的恐惧，但它很快就过去了。</p>
<p>我们来到这里，是为了拥抱荒野，而不是躲避它。</p>
</div>

### Exploring the Island

Over the next few days, we explored every corner of the island. We climbed the central peak, scrambling over rocks and pulling ourselves up by roots and branches. The view from the top was breathtaking: the entire island spread out below us, a patchwork of green forest, brown earth, and blue sea. I could see the reef that encircled the island, the waves breaking white against its hidden barrier, and beyond it, the endless ocean stretching to the curve of the earth.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>接下来的几天里，我们探索了这座岛的每一个角落。</p>
<p>我们攀登中央的山峰，攀过岩石，抓着树根和枝条把自己拉上去。</p>
<p>山顶的景色令人叹为观止：整座岛在我们脚下铺展开来，是一片由绿色森林、褐色土地和蓝色海洋拼成的斑驳图案。</p>
<p>我能看见环绕岛屿的礁石，海浪在它那隐藏的屏障上碎成白色的浪花，而在它之外，是无尽的海洋一直延伸到地球的弧线。</p>
</div>

At the bottom of the peak, we found a natural bridge of rock spanning a narrow gorge. It was a bold sight, and my brother, who had always been the brave one, walked across it without hesitation. I followed, cautious and slow, my heart pounding, my breath short. Branches would brush against my face as I went, and I had to duck under a bent tree trunk that leaned across the path. On the other side, we discovered a small cave with a narrow entrance.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>在山峰底部，我们发现了一座横跨狭窄峡谷的天然石桥。</p>
<p>那景象颇为险峻，而一向勇敢的哥哥毫不犹豫地走了过去。</p>
<p>我跟在后面，谨慎而缓慢，心跳如鼓，呼吸短促。</p>
<p>我走的时候，枝条不时拂过我的脸，我还得在一根斜跨小径的弯曲树干下低头钻过。</p>
<p>在另一侧，我们发现了一个入口狭窄的小洞穴。</p>
</div>

Inside, the air was cool and damp. The walls were smooth, and water dripped from the ceiling into a pool at the centre. A bubble of air rose from the depths and popped at the surface. It felt like stepping into a cellar beneath the earth. My brother pointed to a small bird with a red breast perched on a ledge — a rare breed, he said, uncommon in most parts of the world but common on this island. It was a clue to how little we still knew about this place. We were blind to its secrets, and that was what made it so thrilling.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>里面空气凉爽而潮湿。</p>
<p>洞壁光滑，水从顶部滴落到中央的一汪水潭里。</p>
<p>一个气泡从深处升起，在水面破裂。</p>
<p>感觉就像走进了地下的一个窖穴。</p>
<p>哥哥指着栖在一处岩架上的一只红胸小鸟——他说，这是一个稀有品种，在世界大多数地方都不常见，但在这座岛上却很普通。</p>
<p>这条线索说明我们对这个地方的了解还多么少。</p>
<p>我们对它的秘密一无所知，而正是这一点让它如此令人激动。</p>
</div>

We explored further and found the ruins of the old civilization the book had described. Stone walls, half buried in vine and moss, marked the outline of what had once been a chamber or a temple. A crumbling chimney still stood, a relic of some colonial-era settlement. We found shards of pottery, a rusty iron bolt, the remains of a brick oven, and a small bone — perhaps from a bird or a fish. It was eerie and fascinating, a glimpse into a world that had ceased to exist but had left its mark on the landscape.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们继续深入探索，找到了书中描述的那个古老文明的遗迹。</p>
<p>半埋在藤蔓和苔藓中的石墙，勾勒出这里曾经是一间居室或一座神庙的轮廓。</p>
<p>一座坍塌的烟囱依然立着，是某个殖民时代定居点的遗物。</p>
<p>我们找到了陶器碎片、一枚生锈的铁螺栓、一座砖砌炉灶的残迹，还有一根小骨头——也许是鸟或鱼的。</p>
<p>这既诡异又迷人，让人窥见一个已经不复存在、却在土地上留下痕迹的世界。</p>
</div>

My brother collected a bunch of samples: pottery fragments, a chip of stone with traces of chemical pigment, a coin of uncertain origin. He placed them carefully in a box, labeling each by category. "These belong in a museum," he said. "Or at least in a proper collection. But for now, we are the only ones who know they exist."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>哥哥收集了一堆样本：陶器碎片、一块带有化学颜料痕迹的石片，还有一枚来源不明的硬币。</p>
<p>他小心地把它们放进一个盒子里，按类别逐一贴上标签。</p>
<p>“这些东西应该陈列在博物馆里，”他说。</p>
<p>“至少也该放进一个像样的藏品系列里。</p>
<p>但眼下，只有我们知道它们的存在。”</p>
</div>

### The Cave Paintings

One afternoon, while exploring a larger cave near the shore, we stumbled upon something extraordinary. We entered through a breach in the rock face and found ourselves in a vast chamber. We made a circuit of the walls, and at every turn, there were paintings — crude but powerful images of animals, boats, and human figures. They depicted scenes of hunting and celebration, of battles and ceremonies. One panel showed a chief or a bishop-like figure raising his arms before a crowd. Another showed a bride and groom joining hands in what appeared to be a wedding ceremony. A third depicted a burial, with mourners standing in a circle around a body. In a small cell off the main chamber, we found more paintings — these ones smaller, more intimate, as if meant for private viewing. It was a catalogue of a people's history, painted on stone, preserved by the shelter of the cave.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>一天下午，我们在探索海岸附近一个较大的洞穴时，意外发现了一件非同寻常的东西。</p>
<p>我们从岩壁的一处缺口进入，发现自己置身于一个巨大的洞窟之中。</p>
<p>我们沿着洞壁绕行一圈，每到一处都有壁画——描绘动物、船只和人像的粗犷却有力的图像。</p>
<p>它们描绘了狩猎与庆祝、战斗与仪式的场景。</p>
<p>其中一幅画面上，一个首领或类似主教的人物在一群人面前高举双臂。</p>
<p>另一幅则描绘了一对新人在看似婚礼的仪式中牵手。</p>
<p>第三幅描绘了一场葬礼，哀悼者围成一圈站在一具遗体周围。</p>
<p>在主洞窟旁的一个小侧室里，我们发现更多的壁画——这些更小、更私密，仿佛是为私人观赏而作。</p>
<p>这是一部绘在石头上、被洞穴的庇护保存下来的民族历史图录。</p>
</div>

We took photographs and made careful notes, aware that we were witnessing something rare. My brother examined the pigments and speculated about their composition. He scraped a tiny chip from the wall and placed it in a plastic container. "These could be centuries old," he said, his voice barely above a whisper. "Maybe longer. This is a treasure."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们拍了照片，仔细做了记录，意识到自己正在目睹某种罕见之物。</p>
<p>哥哥仔细查看那些颜料，推测它们的成分。</p>
<p>他从墙上刮下一小片，放进一个塑料容器里。</p>
<p>“这些可能有几百年历史了，”他说，声音低得几乎听不见。</p>
<p>“也许更久。</p>
<p>这是一件珍宝。”</p>
</div>

The discovery gave us a new sense of purpose. We decided to document everything we could, not just for ourselves but on behalf of anyone who might care about preserving this place. We felt a responsibility, a bond with the people who had created this art, even though they were long gone.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>这一发现给了我们一种新的使命感。</p>
<p>我们决定尽己所能记录下一切，不仅为我们自己，也为任何可能关心保护这个地方的人。</p>
<p>我们感到一种责任，一种与那些创作了这些艺术、尽管早已逝去的人们之间的联结。</p>
</div>

That night, we sat by the fire and talked. My brother was convinced that the cave paintings represented a breakthrough in our understanding of the island's history. "This changes everything," he said. "These people had a complex civilization. They were not just surviving — they were thriving. Every citizen in that community must have played a part."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>那天晚上，我们坐在火边聊天。</p>
<p>哥哥确信，这些洞穴壁画代表着我们对这座岛历史认识上的一次突破。</p>
<p>“这改变了一切，”他说。</p>
<p>“这些人拥有一个复杂的文明。</p>
<p>他们不仅仅是在勉强生存——他们是在繁荣兴盛。</p>
<p>那个社群里的每一个成员一定都扮演了自己的角色。”</p>
</div>

"And now they are gone," I said. "Like everyone, eventually."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“而现在他们都消失了，”我说。</p>
<p>“就像每个人一样，终有那么一天。”</p>
</div>

"Do not be so bitter about it," he replied. "They left something behind. That is more than most people do. We should commemorate them, not mourn them."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“别这么愤懑，”他回答。</p>
<p>“他们留下了些什么。</p>
<p>这比大多数人都做得更多。</p>
<p>我们应该纪念他们，而不是哀悼他们。”</p>
</div>

He was right, of course. The paintings were a blessing, a gift from the past. They reminded us that **human beings have always sought to communicate**, to leave a mark, to say: we were here. It was a humbling thought, and it gave me a new clarity about our own place in the world.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>当然，他说得对。</p>
<p>这些壁画是一种福泽，是来自过去的馈赠。</p>
<p>它们提醒我们，人类始终在寻求沟通、留下印记，说一声：我们曾在这里。</p>
<p>这是一个令人谦卑的想法，它让我对自身在世界中的位置有了新的清晰认识。</p>
</div>

## Chapter Four: The Storm

Life on the island settled into a rhythm. We woke with the sun, ate a simple breakfast, and spent the day exploring. We learned to read the weather by watching the clouds — the tall, white columns that meant fair weather, the dark, heavy masses that warned of a storm. We learned which plants were beneficial and which could cause harm. We learned to behave with caution around the wildlife, observing their behaviour without disturbing them. Nature was our teacher, and we were willing students.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>岛上的生活渐渐有了节奏。</p>
<p>我们随太阳一同醒来，吃一顿简单的早餐，然后花一整天去探索。</p>
<p>我们学会通过观察云来解读天气——那些高耸的白柱意味着好天气，那些暗沉厚重的云团则预示着暴风雨。</p>
<p>我们弄清了哪些植物有益，哪些可能有害。</p>
<p>我们学会了在野生动物周围保持谨慎，观察它们的习性而不去打扰。</p>
<p>大自然是我们的老师，而我们是心甘情愿的学生。</p>
</div>

The climate was warm and humid, characteristic of a tropical region. Insects buzzed around our camp — one bit me on the chest, leaving a red mark. A small bug crawled across my notebook as I wrote. My brother told me not to complain. "It is part of the experience," he said.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>气候温暖潮湿，是典型的热带地区特征。</p>
<p>昆虫在我们营地周围嗡嗡作响——有一只叮了我的胸口，留下一个红印。</p>
<p>我写字的时候，一只小虫子爬过我的笔记本。</p>
<p>哥哥叫我不许抱怨。</p>
<p>“这也是体验的一部分，”他说。</p>
</div>

### The Storm Breaks

One evening, a storm hit. Black clouds gathered on the horizon, and the wind began to blow with a brutal force. Rain hammered down on our tent. Lightning flashed, and thunder followed with a deep bass rumble that seemed to come from the very bottom of the earth. The blast shook the ground. We huddled in our shelter, our hearts pounding, barely breathing, listening to the fury outside. It was like standing on a battlefield, and for a moment I thought the tent would collapse entirely.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>一天傍晚，暴风雨来袭。</p>
<p>乌云在地平线上聚集，风开始以狂暴的力量刮起。</p>
<p>雨水猛烈地砸在我们的帐篷上。</p>
<p>闪电划过，接着是雷声，一阵深沉的隆隆低响，仿佛来自大地的最深处。</p>
<p>那炸裂的巨响震动了地面。</p>
<p>我们蜷缩在帐篷里，心跳如鼓，几乎不敢呼吸，听着外面的狂暴。</p>
<p>就像站在战场上一样，有一瞬间我以为帐篷会彻底塌掉。</p>
</div>

At one point, the wind tore a corner of our tent loose, and rain poured in. We grabbed our belongings and scrambled for the cave, running through the dark with water streaming down our faces. I could feel the blood draining from my cheeks. My brother stumbled and cut his hand on a rock; it began to bleed, but he did not stop. We pressed on, driven by a survival instinct that bypassed the brain and went straight to the muscles.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>有一刻，风把帐篷的一角掀开了，雨水灌了进来。</p>
<p>我们抓起随身物品，跌跌撞撞地向洞穴奔去，在黑暗中奔跑，水顺着我们的脸往下淌。</p>
<p>我能感觉到血色从我的脸颊上褪去。</p>
<p>哥哥绊了一下，手在石头上划破了；血开始流，但他没有停下。</p>
<p>我们继续向前，被一种绕过大脑、直抵肌肉的求生本能驱使着。</p>
</div>

The cave offered refuge — a buffer against the storm. We sat in its dry interior, wrapped in our blankets, and waited. My brother managed to light a candle and then a small fire with some dry wood from his backpack. The flames began to burn steadily, casting a warm glow on the walls. We boiled water for coffee. The warmth was a comfort, and gradually the fear faded. I bound a strip of cloth around his hand to stop the bleeding. He winced briefly but did not complain.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>洞穴提供了庇护——一道抵御暴风雨的缓冲。</p>
<p>我们坐在干燥的洞里，裹着毯子等待。</p>
<p>哥哥设法点起了一支蜡烛，又用背包里的一些干柴生起了一小堆火。</p>
<p>火焰开始稳定地燃烧，在洞壁上投下温暖的光。</p>
<p>我们烧水煮咖啡。</p>
<p>这份温暖是一种慰藉，恐惧也渐渐消退。</p>
<p>我用一条布条缠住他的手，止住了血。</p>
<p>他短暂地皱了下眉，但没有抱怨。</p>
</div>

"This is quite an adventure," he said, pouring coffee into a bowl.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“这还真是一场冒险，”他说着，把咖啡倒进一只碗里。</p>
</div>

"I would call it more of a misadventure," I replied. "But at least we are not bored."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“我倒更愿意称之为一场倒霉的经历，”我回答。</p>
<p>“但至少我们不无聊。”</p>
</div>

He laughed. "You could not be boring if you tried."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>他笑了。</p>
<p>“你就是想无聊也做不到。”</p>
</div>

We sat in silence for a while, listening to the storm. I thought about how quickly nature could turn from gentle to fierce. It was a reminder of our own fragility, of how small we were in the grand scheme of things. Anyone who boasts of conquering nature is a fool, I thought. Nature does not care about our bold plans or our big ambitions. It simply is, and we must learn to live within it, not against it.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们静静地坐了一会儿，听着暴风雨。</p>
<p>我想着大自然从温和转为狂暴是多么迅速。</p>
<p>这提醒着我们自身的脆弱，提醒我们在这宏大的格局中是多么渺小。</p>
<p>任何夸口要征服自然的人都是傻瓜，我想。</p>
<p>大自然并不在乎我们大胆的计划或宏大的抱负。</p>
<p>它就是它本身，我们必须学会与它共存，而不是与它对抗。</p>
</div>

"I bet the captain would have something to say about this," I said.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“我敢打赌船长对此会有一番话说，”我说。</p>
</div>

"He would probably tell us it was character-building," my brother replied. "He has that kind of broadly optimistic outlook."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“他大概会告诉我们这是在锤炼性格，”哥哥回答。</p>
<p>“他就有那种宽泛乐观的心态。”</p>
</div>

"He would be right," I said. "We dodged a bullet out there. Things could have been much worse."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“他会是对的，”我说。</p>
<p>“我们躲过了一劫。</p>
<p>情况本来可能糟糕得多。”</p>
</div>

### After the Storm

By morning, the storm had passed. We emerged to find the forest drenched but gleaming. Sunlight broke through the remaining clouds, and beams of light fell through the canopy. The air was clean and fresh, washed of all dust and heat. Birds were singing again, and a butterfly drifted past on fragile wings. A buck stood at the edge of the clearing, watching us with dark eyes before turning and disappearing into the bush. It was, I thought, one of the most beautiful things I had ever seen.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>到早晨，暴风雨已经过去。</p>
<p>我们走出来，只见森林湿透了却闪着光。</p>
<p>阳光穿透残留的云层，一束束光从树冠间洒落。</p>
<p>空气洁净清新，所有尘埃和暑气都被洗去了。</p>
<p>鸟儿又唱起了歌，一只蝴蝶扇着脆弱的翅膀飘过。</p>
<p>一头雄鹿站在空地的边缘，用深色的眼睛看着我们，随后转身消失在灌木丛中。</p>
<p>我想，这是我所见过的最美的事物之一。</p>
</div>

## Chapter Five: The Return

Our last day on the island came too soon. We packed our gear, cleaned our campsite, and made sure we left nothing behind. The captain arrived at noon, as promised, and we boarded the boat for the return trip. As the island receded behind us, I felt a pang of sadness — a sense that I was leaving something important behind. But I also felt a deep satisfaction. We had come seeking adventure, and we had found it. We had challenged ourselves and discovered capabilities we did not know we had. We had witnessed nature in its rawest, most powerful form, and we had been changed by it.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我们在岛上的最后一天来得太快了。</p>
<p>我们收拾好装备，清理了营地，确保没有留下任何东西。</p>
<p>船长如约在正午到达，我们登上船踏上归程。</p>
<p>当岛屿在我们身后退去，我感到一阵心酸——一种把重要之物留在身后的感觉。</p>
<p>但我也有一种深深的满足。</p>
<p>我们来时是为了寻求冒险，而我们找到了。</p>
<p>我们挑战了自己，发现了自己不知道拥有的能力。</p>
<p>我们目睹了大自然最原始、最强大的形态，并因之而改变。</p>
</div>

On the bus ride back to the city, my brother turned to me. "You know," he said, "I think this was the best trip of my life."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>在返回城市的公交车上，哥哥转向我。</p>
<p>“你知道吗，”他说，“我觉得这是我这辈子最好的一次旅行。”</p>
</div>

I nodded. "Mine too."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我点了点头。</p>
<p>“我也是。”</p>
</div>

### Sharing the Story

He pulled out his phone and opened his browser. "I am going to put these photographs on my blog," he said. "People should see this place. They should know it exists."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>他掏出手机，打开了浏览器。</p>
<p>“我要把这些照片放到我的博客上，”他说。</p>
<p>“人们应该看看这个地方。</p>
<p>他们应该知道它存在。”</p>
</div>

"That is a good idea," I said. "But let us also think about how we can help protect it. This island deserves more than just visitors. It deserves a champion."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“这是个好主意，”我说。</p>
<p>“但我们也该想想怎么帮着保护它。</p>
<p>这座岛值得的不只是游客。</p>
<p>它值得一位守护者。”</p>
</div>

He agreed, and we spent the rest of the ride talking about what we might do. We could approach a commission or a government committee — perhaps even a commissioner or a commander with authority over environmental protection. We could commit to a long-term effort, a real commitment: raise awareness, contribute to a charity, launch a campaign. We could try to get a company involved, or compare our findings with other expeditions and publish a comparison. We would have to compete for attention and funding, but the cause was worth it. My brother texted his boyfriend to say we were safe, then turned back to me.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>他同意了，剩下的路程我们都在谈论可以做些什么。</p>
<p>我们可以去找一个委员会或政府的一个专门委员会——也许甚至是某位掌管环境保护的专员或长官。</p>
<p>我们可以投入一项长期的努力，一份真正的承诺：提高公众意识、向一家慈善机构捐款、发起一场运动。</p>
<p>我们可以尝试让一家公司参与进来，或者把我们的发现与其他考察队的成果做比较，并发表一份对比。</p>
<p>我们得为关注度和资金去竞争，但这项事业值得。</p>
<p>哥哥给他男朋友发了条短信报平安，然后又转回头对着我。</p>
</div>

"The bulk of the work will be communication," he said. "We need to get the community involved. This should not just be about us. We need a breakdown of the findings — something clear and accessible."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“大部分工作在于沟通，”他说。</p>
<p>“我们得让社区参与进来。</p>
<p>这不该只是关于我们。</p>
<p>我们需要把发现成果做一份明细——要清楚、易懂。”</p>
</div>

"Exactly," I said. "We need to combine our resources. Maybe we could bid for a grant, or apply for some kind of certificate of recognition. We are capable of doing this. We have the capacity. But we have to choose our approach carefully. We must not betray the island's location to the wrong people. There is always a bias toward development, and we cannot let that happen here."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“没错，”我说。</p>
<p>“我们得把资源整合起来。</p>
<p>也许我们可以竞标一笔拨款，或者申请某种认可证书。</p>
<p>我们有能力做到。</p>
<p>我们有这个实力。</p>
<p>但我们得谨慎选择做法。</p>
<p>我们绝不能把这岛的位置泄露给不该知道的人。</p>
<p>人们总是偏向开发，我们绝不能让它在这里发生。”</p>
</div>

He nodded. "I beg you to be patient. These things take time."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>他点了点头。</p>
<p>“我求你要有耐心。</p>
<p>这些事急不得。”</p>
</div>

"I know," I said. "But when we succeed, I will be the first to cheer."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“我知道，”我说。</p>
<p>“但等我们成功时，我会第一个欢呼。”</p>
</div>

"Let us do it," he said. "Let us tell the story."

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>“我们就这么干吧，”他说。</p>
<p>“让我们把这个故事讲出去。”</p>
</div>

### Home, Changed

Back home, life resumed its normal pace. I returned to my job, my brother to his. But something had changed in both of us. We carried the island with us — not just in our photographs and notes, but in our hearts. The memory of that wild, beloved place had become a part of who we were.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>回到家后，生活恢复了平常的节奏。</p>
<p>我回到了我的工作，哥哥回到了他的。</p>
<p>但我们俩身上都有些东西变了。</p>
<p>我们把这岛随身带着——不只是在照片和笔记里，更是在心里。</p>
<p>那个原始而可爱的地方的记忆，已经成为我们自身的一部分。</p>
</div>

I often thought about the cave paintings and the people who had made them. They had lived and died on that island, and all that remained of them was a handful of images on a cave wall. But those images were enough. They told us that these people had loved and celebrated, had fought and prayed, had born children and buried their dead with ceremony. They had been, in every sense, human beings — and their story, though forgotten for centuries, had not been lost.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我常常想起那些洞穴壁画和创作它们的人们。</p>
<p>他们在那座岛上生活、死去，留给世人的只有洞壁上寥寥几幅图像。</p>
<p>但那些图像已经足够。</p>
<p>它们告诉我们，这些人曾经相爱、庆祝，曾经战斗、祈祷，曾经生儿育女、以仪式埋葬死者。</p>
<p>无论从哪方面说，他们都是活生生的人——而他们的故事，虽被遗忘数百年，却并未失落。</p>
</div>

The island had made us beneficiaries of that story, and we had a duty to pass it on. I thought about my brother, my girlfriend, my colleagues — all the people I loved — and I felt a sense of gratitude so deep it was almost a physical sensation in my chest. To be alive, to be breathing, to be part of this extraordinary world: that was the greatest gift of all.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>这座岛让我们成了那个故事的受益者，而我们也有责任把它传递下去。</p>
<p>我想着哥哥、女朋友、同事们——所有我爱的人——感到一种深到几乎在胸口成为实体感受的感激。</p>
<p>活着，呼吸着，成为这个非凡世界的一部分：那才是最伟大的馈赠。</p>
</div>

And sometimes, on a quiet evening, when the city is asleep and the sky is clear, I close my eyes and I am back on that beach, with the sand between my toes and the sound of the waves in my ears. I feel the breeze on my cheek, and I smell the salt and the flowers, and I am at peace. The island taught me that adventure is not just about going somewhere new. It is about opening your eyes to the wonder that exists in every corner of this world, if only we have the courage to seek it.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>有时，在一个安静的夜晚，当城市沉睡、天空晴朗，我闭上眼，就回到了那片海滩，脚趾间是沙，耳边是海浪声。</p>
<p>我感到微风拂过脸颊，闻到咸味与花香，内心一片安宁。</p>
<p>这座岛教会我，冒险不只是去往一个新的地方。</p>
<p>而是要睁大眼睛去看这个世界每一个角落里存在的奇妙，只要我们敢于去寻找。</p>
</div>

That, I believe, is the true benefit of travel. Not the miles you cover, or the photographs you take, but the way it changes you — quietly, deeply, permanently. We went to the island looking for adventure, and we found something better: **a sense of belonging to something bigger than ourselves**. And that, in the end, is worth more than any treasure.

<div class="tk-trans">
<p>我相信，这才是旅行真正的益处。</p>
<p>不是你所走过的里程，也不是你拍下的照片，而是它改变你的方式——安静地、深刻地、永久地。</p>
<p>我们去那座岛是为了寻找冒险，却找到了更好的东西：一种归属于比自身更宏大之物的感觉。</p>
<p>而这，归根结底，比任何珍宝都更珍贵。</p>
</div>
